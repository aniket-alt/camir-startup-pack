## CAMIR makes the self-hosted inference frontier auditable

**San Jose, September 2031** — CAMIR today announced that platform teams can publish a reproducible cost-quality frontier for their own open-weight model pool, including judge agreement, truncation and parse-failure rates, utilization assumptions and the endpoint owners who accepted the quality tolerance.

The standard is deliberately not a claim that CAMIR's router is more accurate. It is a record of what a pool can do, what its evaluation harness may be hiding, and when routing should not be deployed.

## The metric the market adopted

Teams now ask for a **frontier run**, not an unsupported cost-reduction headline. A frontier run includes the fixed-model baseline, oracle ceiling, route, quality tolerance, self-hosted cost parameters, judge set, inter-judge agreement and artifact decomposition. A run that cannot be reproduced is labelled not reproducible.

The market adopted this language because published routing numbers vary by benchmark: RouteLLM reports 3.66x on MT-Bench, 1.41x on MMLU and 1.49x on GSM8K [S2]. It also adopted artifact reporting after evidence of truncation in 65% of MMLU and 57% of MedQA cases and 5–12% parse failures on MMLU [S5].

## How we got here

1. **2026:** SJSU CMPE 295A capstone team publishes the first CAMIR brief, explicitly pre-traction, and tests whether a self-hosted oracle ceiling exists.
2. **2026–2027:** CAMIR ships the open harness, cascade route, artifact guard and customer-perimeter cost derivation. It publishes null results where the ceiling is too low.
3. **2027–2028:** Platform teams adopt endpoint-owned tolerance, tier-stamped traces, shadow mode and unilateral pin-to-large as deployment requirements.
4. **2028–2030:** The control plane measures model upgrades, cache residuals and utilization without claiming those levers are already a product-market fit.
5. **2031:** A frontier run becomes the audit object that connects inference spend, quality risk and the decision to route.
