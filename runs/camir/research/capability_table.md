# CAMIR — Enabling Capability Table

**What this is** — the state of the art, as of September 2026, in every technology CAMIR depends on: small-model capability, multi-model serving, routing signals, judging, and cost curves — each with a measured number, a cost trend, its fit to CAMIR's mechanism, and the gap that remains.
**Why it exists** — `BRIEF.md` asserts three "why now" shifts and explicitly flags them as needing dates and citations (assumption A12). An undated "why now" is the first claim an investor falsifies, and two of the three shifts turn out to be **weaker than asserted**: self-hosting rarely wins on cost alone against budget open-weight APIs [S28], and the tier cost spread that makes routing pay is *compressing*, not widening. This table is where that gets said before a partner meeting says it.
**How to read it** — the Gap column is the honest part; read it first. Rows C1–C3 are the "why now" evidence, C4–C7 are what CAMIR builds on, C8–C10 are what constrains it. §Verdict on "why now" grades each of the founder's three claimed shifts.
**Depends on / feeds** — depends on [sources.md](sources.md); feeds [survey.md](survey.md), [../tech/whitepaper.md](../tech/whitepaper.md) §2, [../tech/deep_dives.md](../tech/deep_dives.md), [../tech/not_vaporware.md](../tech/not_vaporware.md) and the "why now" slide of [../narrative/pitch_deck.md](../narrative/pitch_deck.md).

| **C5** | **Routing signal — surface features** | Classifier routers, 2023–2026 families [S4][S6] | 21 routing methods across 5 benchmarks converge to a **narrow accuracy band far below oracle**; cause is a **predictability bottleneck** — routers learn globally averaged model-performance trends, not query-specific signal. Best remedies (more data, stronger encoders, encoder fine-tuning) gain **up to 2.13 percentage points** [S4] | Router serving cost small and falling: $3.19–$39.26 per million requests by router class [S1] | **This is CAMIR's classifier route, and the evidence is against it as a source of advantage** | The gap is the finding itself. Surface-feature difficulty prediction appears to be near its ceiling. CAMIR must not claim classifier superiority; it can only claim to *measure where the plateau sits for a given pool* |
| **C6** | **Routing signal — calibrated uncertainty (post-generation)** | UCCI and cascade-deferral literature [S8] | **Simple confidence measures route as well as trained routing models** [S8]. With C1's compressing tier spread, the arithmetic is much tighter than FrugalGPT's 2023 hosted-price setting. **Nobody has published cascade economics under compressed self-hosted spreads** [G2] |
| **C7** | **Routing signal — prefill activations** | LLM Router: Rethinking Routing with Prefill Activations [S10] | Routing on internal prefill activations rather than surface features (2026) | Marginal — activations already computed during prefill | **The one signal class that is structurally exclusive to self-hosters.** A hosted router cannot see activations without ceasing to be hosted | Early-stage research. Requires serving-engine integration, so it is a vLLM/SGLang patch rather than a proxy feature — a real engineering commitment. **Highest-upside, highest-risk technical direction available to CAMIR** |
| **C8** | **Correctness judging at scale** | LLM-as-judge, evaluated systematically 2026 [S33][S34] | ~**80% agreement with human preferences**, matching human-human consistency, but **inter-judge agreement only ~76%**; judges individually consistent yet mutually inconsistent, so **judge choice may damage validity more than within-judge randomness** [S33]. ## Verdict on the founder's three "why now" shifts

Assumption A12 asked for these to be dated and cited, or dropped. Grading each:

| Claimed shift | Verdict | Evidence | What has to change in the pitch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Capable small/mid open-weight models are now genuinely usable** | **Confirmed, with a sting** | Gemma 4 31B-thinking within ~10 Elo of 600B–1000B+ frontier at ~10× fewer parameters, April 2026 [S29] | Keep it, and **own the sting**: the same convergence compresses the cost spread routing arbitrages. State it as "a real small tier now exists" — not as "the gap is widening", which is false |
| **2. Serving multiple models locally is now practical** | **Partly confirmed** | Multi-LoRA consolidation with megabyte-scale per-model overhead [S31]; SGLang first-class multi-adapter batching [S32]; Ollama ~100K → **52M monthly downloads** Q1 2023 → Q1 2026 [S30] | **Weaken the claim.** Multi-LoRA shares one base model; CAMIR's tiers are distinct base models needing separate resident VRAM. And downloads are not production deployments [G1]. Say "multi-model serving is practical", not "free" |
| **3. Inference became a production line item** | **Confirmed, and the strongest** | Inference is the **second-largest line item in enterprise AI budgets in 2026**, behind talent; 55–80% of enterprise AI GPU spend [S25] | Keep, and **lead with it**. It is the only one of the three that creates a *buyer* rather than a *capability*. Cite as directional analyst estimate, not as a sized market |

**Two shifts the founder did not name, both of which matter more than shift #2:**

- **4. Routing became free and default for hosted-catalog users, in 2026** [S20][S21]. This resets the price expectation and simultaneously produced the public proof that vendor-set tolerance fails visibly. It is the sharpest "why now" available, and it is a *competitive* shift rather than a technical one.
- **5. The field published its own ceiling, in mid-2026** [S4][S5][S15]. The routing plateau and the unsolvability-ceiling results appeared within a month of each other and together relocate the open problem from *algorithm* to *measurement*. A project starting today can aim at the newly identified gap; a project started in 2024 could not have known it existed.

## What is not yet possible

Named explicitly, because a capability table that lists only green lights is a brochure.

1. **Reliable pre-generation difficulty prediction.** [S4] is the field's own verdict. CAMIR's classifier route is bounded by it, and this is assumption A1.
2. **A trustworthy single-judge quality axis.** ~76% inter-judge agreement and 30% verdict flips at temperature 1 [S33][S34] mean no single judge can carry a published frontier alone.
3. **Comparable cross-paper routing results.** [S13]. Every reported saving is conditioned on an unstated harness.
4. **A cost axis for self-hosted pools.** RouterBench prices against hosted APIs [S7]; amortised-GPU-hour costing is unbuilt. **This is the most concrete, most achievable open contribution on the table.**
5. **Measured residual savings after caching** [G4].

## Recommended next 3

1. **Make judging protocol a first-class deliverable, not an implementation detail.** [S33][S34] force temperature 0, fixed position, verbosity control and published inter-judge agreement. This becomes a named component in `tech/deep_dives.md` and a differentiator, because no competitor publishes it.
2. **Build the self-hosted cost axis and publish it as a standalone contribution.** [S7] plus [S26][S27] make it tractable, [C10]'s gap makes it novel, and it is useful to the field even if CAMIR's router plateaus.
3. **Replace "why now" shift #2 with the free-and-default shift** [S20][S21] in every narrative artifact. It is better evidenced, more urgent, and it is the shift that creates the wedge rather than merely permitting the build.
