# CAMIR — Algorithm deep dives

**What this is** — the eight tier-1 algorithms CAMIR has to implement correctly, each specified as: the one question it answers, the named methods that answer it, its inputs and outputs, the design choice that could reasonably have gone the other way, and how it fails.
**Why it exists** — CAMIR's technical claim is a *measurement* claim, and measurement code fails silently. A miscalibrated gate does not crash, it quietly escalates 70% of traffic and turns the cascade into a more expensive way to serve identical requests; a judge left at temperature 1 does not error, it produces a frontier whose test-retest reliability is ~70% [S34] and every downstream tolerance policy is set against noise. This document is where the failure modes are named before they are shipped, and where "we use calibrated uncertainty" is forced to become a specific estimator with a specific fitting set.
**How to read it** — DD1 and DD3 carry the pack's contribution and should be attacked first; DD2 is where the money is; DD4 is where the number is most easily fudged and is the one an adversarial reviewer should re-derive by hand. Every item's **Failure modes** row is the part that matters more than its approach.
**Depends on / feeds** — depends on [../research/capability_table.md](../research/capability_table.md), [../research/survey.md](../research/survey.md), [techniques/wave1.md](techniques/wave1.md), [techniques/wave2.md](techniques/wave2.md), [techniques/wave3.md](techniques/wave3.md), [whitepaper.md](whitepaper.md) §2; feeds [architecture/00_INDEX.md](architecture/00_INDEX.md), [not_vaporware.md](not_vaporware.md), [techniques/decision_tree.md](techniques/decision_tree.md) and [../product/features_flagship.md](../product/features_flagship.md).

**Status: nothing here has been built or measured.** Method choices are drawn from the cited literature; every parameter is `(assumption)` until CAMIR's own benchmark runs [G2].

## DD1 — Oracle ceiling probe and artifact-vs-capability decomposition

**Goal.** Bound everything a router on this pool could ever claim, and split the gap between *this small tier genuinely cannot* and *our harness did not let it*.

**Algorithmic approach.**
1. **Exhaustive cross-tier evaluation** (W1-43): every request in the pinned corpus is generated by *every* tier. Not sampled, not modelled — the ceiling is a fact about the corpus and it costs `|corpus| × |tiers|` full generations.
2. **Oracle-ceiling computation** (W1-44): with per-request correctness labels for every tier, the oracle assigns each request to the cheapest tier that answered correctly. The ceiling is that assignment's cost; the **fixed-model baseline** (W1-45) is the ceiling's counterpart at the top. Everything a router can claim lives strictly between them (PR7).
3. **The decomposition, run twice** — this is the contribution. **Guarded**: generous generation budgets with **finish-reason logging and truncation counting** (W2-34), a **generation-budget sensitivity sweep** (W2-35), **grammar-constrained decoding** (W1-41) or schema extraction with **explicit parse-failure counting** (W1-42), and length-controlled scoring (W2-29). **Unguarded**: the fixed-budget, free-text, loosely-parsed configuration that most published evaluation uses. The difference is the **artifact-vs-capability decomposition of the oracle gap** (W2-37), re-scored as W2-38.
4. **Co-failure structure estimation** (W2-11): correlation of correctness across tiers, because failures overlap and the ceiling is lower than tier independence would predict [S15].
5. **Non-nested tier set** (PR10): the requests where a smaller tier was right and a larger wrong — the evidence that routing is per-request assignment, not downgrading [S3].

**Inputs.** Pinned corpus (DD8), `pool_manifest`, judge configuration (DD3).
**Outputs.** `frontier_run.ceiling_guarded`, `.ceiling_unguarded`, `.artifact_share`, `.cofailure_matrix`, `.non_nested_set`, the disqualification verdict.

**Key design choice — the ceiling is computed exhaustively, not estimated.** An IRT or matrix-factorisation model over the correctness matrix (W2-7, W2-8) could predict unobserved query-model pairs at a fraction of the cost, and CAMIR still uses **low-rank completion** (W2-9) for incremental re-scoring. But the *first* ceiling on a new pool is exhaustive, because a modelled ceiling inherits the model's error and the ceiling's whole job is to be the thing nobody argues with. It is the number a disqualification rests on, and a disqualification is CAMIR telling a paying prospect to leave — that number cannot be an estimate.

**Failure modes and fallbacks.**

| Failure | Symptom | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| Corpus unrepresentative of the hard tail | Ceiling flattering; production escalation far above shadow | DD8's per-stratum error bars (W1-48) make it visible; re-stratify by oracle label (W1-47) and re-run |
| Judge is the bottleneck, not the tier | Ceiling tracks judge leniency, not capability | Inter-judge agreement is on the same page (DD3); agreement below the ~0.76 field baseline [S33] marks the run not reproducible |
| Guarded run is *itself* over-fit to the guard | Artifact share inflated because generous budgets let the small tier ramble into a lucky answer | Length-controlled scoring (W2-29) plus a budget sweep — the artifact share must be stable across budgets, not monotonic in them |
| **Cost blows the wall clock** | The probe is O(corpus × tiers) full generations and is the only step that can break G1 | Pre-flight estimate refuses runs that will not finish in a stated budget; offer a smaller stratified corpus ([../product/ux_spec.md](../product/ux_spec.md) S1) |
| Artifact share comes back ~0 on a well-instrumented pool | The pack's thesis looks weakened | **Correct behaviour, and expected**: Wen's gap is 6pp against Priya's 19 ([../product/journeys/edge_high.md](../product/journeys/edge_high.md)). The effect *should* shrink as harness quality rises — a share that did not would be suspicious |

## DD2 — The confidence gate

**Goal.** Given a small-tier answer, escalate or return — at a threshold that maps to a *declared* tolerance rather than a hand-tuned constant.

**Algorithmic approach.**
1. **The score.** Not one estimator, a stack, because they fail differently: **length-normalised sequence log-likelihood** (W1-21) as the cheap default; **predictive entropy** and **margin** (W1-19, W1-18); **semantic entropy** (W1-23) where meaning-equivalent samples must not be counted as disagreement; **self-consistency agreement rate** (W1-22) where the budget allows k samples; and a **learned answer scorer** (W1-4) — a DistilBERT-class regression head over `(query, answer)` — which is FrugalGPT's actual mechanism [S3] and is distinct from the generator's own confidence.
2. **The calibration.** Raw scores are not probabilities. **Temperature scaling** (W1-12) first because it is one parameter and does not distort ranking; **isotonic regression** (W1-14) when the calibration set is large enough to afford it; **reliability diagrams and adaptive-ECE** (W1-15) reported, not just computed.
3. **The rule.** **Chow's rule** (W1-7) is the formal ground: under a fixed rejection cost, thresholding calibrated confidence *is* the risk-optimal deferral rule. The threshold is then set by **conformal risk control** (W2-2, W2-3), which converts "I want at most 1% quality loss versus baseline" into a threshold with a **distribution-free finite-sample guarantee** — the only technique in the stack that turns a tolerance into a promise rather than a hope.
4. **Ongoing validity.** **Adaptive conformal inference** (W2-5) maintains coverage as traffic drifts; **weighted conformal under covariate shift** (W2-6) handles the post-cache case, where a semantic cache upstream has removed 20–45% of traffic and adversely selected the remainder toward the hard end [S36].
5. **Reported as a curve, never a point.** **Risk–coverage curves** (W1-8) are the selective-prediction form of the frontier, and **escalation-rate targeting** (W1-6) is the operator-facing control variable — because the operator's real question is "what fraction of my traffic hits the large tier", not "what is τ".

**Inputs.** Small-tier answer + logits/samples, endpoint `tolerance_policy`, calibration set from held-out `judgment_record` rows.
**Outputs.** Escalate/return, the calibrated score, and the threshold version — all onto the `decision_record` and the caller's span.

**Key design choice — the threshold is a versioned object fitted to a declared tolerance, not a constant.** PR2 says the deferral rule matters more than either predictor, since it sets the entire cost/coverage trade-off. A constant compiled into the router cannot be audited, cannot be attributed when quality moves, and cannot be owned by the person bearing the risk — which is the whole of Ravi's veto.

**Failure modes and fallbacks.**

| Failure | Symptom | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| **Escalation rate high enough to invert the economics** | Cascade costs *more* than dispatching to the large tier: failed small attempt + gate + large answer (PR1) | Escalation-rate accounting shows the decomposition **before** enforcement; the break-even line is drawn on the live view. Above it, CAMIR recommends the classifier route or no routing |
| Calibration set too small | Overfit threshold; shadow-to-production gap | Refuse to fit below a stated count and say the count: *"340 labelled examples, minimum 1,000"* |
| Confidence is well-calibrated but uninformative | Gate performs at chance; scores cluster | Fall back to cascade-with-fixed-escalation-budget (W1-6), which still saves if the tiers are cheap enough, and report the gate as non-informative rather than hiding it |
| **Adversarial suppression** | Escalation spikes on one key; quality unchanged, bill rises — a forced-deferral attack [S9] | **Escalation-rate anomaly detection** (W3-16) ships *with* the gate, plus per-tenant escalation budgets (W3-15) and randomised thresholds (W3-17). O5 |
| Calibrated confidence beats the trained classifier | DD5 shows no lift | **Expected** [S8]. Cascade stays primary; the ablation is reported honestly (PR3) |

## DD3 — The judge harness

**Goal.** Produce a correctness label that a different team could reproduce.

**Algorithmic approach.** A protocol, not a model choice — **forced** by the 2026 reliability results and not configurable:

1. **Temperature 0, with test-retest measured and published** (W2-27). Same-verdict rates exceed 95% at temperature 0 and fall to ~70% at temperature 1 [S34]. A configurable temperature will be set to 1 by someone, so it is not configurable.
2. **Position-permutation averaging** (W2-28), or a fixed position where averaging is unaffordable. Position bias produces ~40% GPT-4 inconsistency [S34].
3. **Length-controlled scoring** (W2-29). Verbosity bias inflates ~15% [S34]. Its direction across tiers is **a hypothesis, not a theorem** — if it favours longer large-tier answers it biases the ceiling downward, but that varies by task, format and response style, so CAMIR reports it per tier and per task rather than assuming a sign ([whitepaper.md](whitepaper.md) §1, F3).
4. **Verification before judgment wherever the task admits it**: **exact-match / normalised string match** (W1-39), **programmatic unit-test execution** (W1-40), **schema extraction with parse-failure counting** (W1-42). A judged label is the fallback, not the default — the cheapest way to raise judge reliability is to need the judge less often.
5. **Multi-judge panels** (W2-30) with **inter-judge agreement published** on every `frontier_run` (W2-31), against the ~76% field baseline [S33]. **Self-enhancement-bias control** (W2-33): a judge never scores its own family unbalanced against another's — self-enhancement runs 5–7% [S34].
6. **Human anchor-set calibration** (W2-32): a small human-labelled set per deployment, which is what turns agreement from a consistency statistic into a validity one. [S33]'s core finding is *reliability without validity* — judges individually consistent yet mutually inconsistent.

**Inputs.** `(request, tier, answer)` triples; task-type declaration; judge set.
**Outputs.** `judgment_record`: per-judge verdict, agreement, truncation flag, parse status, verbosity-normalised score.

**Key design choice — CAMIR ships a protocol and an interface, not a judge.** Wen brings hers ([../product/journeys/edge_high.md](../product/journeys/edge_high.md)); the value is that **CAMIR's code computes her agreement statistic**, not the plug-in's. A vendor asserting its own judge's quality is the same shape as a vendor asserting its own savings [S17].

**Failure modes and fallbacks.** Single-judge degradation → run continues, every downstream artifact stamped *inter-judge agreement unavailable — not reproducible* [S13]. Systematic judge error → the danger [S33] names explicitly, since judge *choice* may damage validity more than stochastic error within one judge; the human anchor set is the only detector, and its absence is a stated limitation. Cost → judging can exceed generation cost on a large corpus; mitigated by preferring programmatic verification and by judging a stratified subsample with per-stratum error bars (W1-48).

## DD4 — The self-hosted cost axis

**Goal.** Price one request on a pool of GPUs the customer already owns — the derivation no published benchmark has done [S7][G2].

**Algorithmic approach.**
1. **Amortised GPU-hour to per-token costing** (W2-39): `cost = GPU-seconds × hourly_rate ÷ utilisation × all_in_multiplier`.
2. **Per-tier resident-VRAM accounting** (W2-42). A tier that must stay resident costs even when idle. Multi-LoRA adapters sharing a base model cost megabytes each [S31]; **distinct base models do not share weights that way**, and conflating the two is the most common way a tier registry lies about cost.
3. **The all-in multiplier** (W2-41), declared not hidden: realistic all-in cost is **3–5× raw GPU rental** once engineering time is counted [S27].
4. **Utilisation-sensitivity sweep** (W2-40). A card idle at 10% utilisation costs **10× per token** [S27]. This parameter moves the answer more than any routing improvement will.
5. **Marginal-cost accounting for escalation** (W2-43): an escalated request pays the small attempt *and* the large answer, so the cascade's marginal cost is not the large tier's cost.
6. **Cost-per-request as the reported unit** (W3-32), imported from FinOps, because it is the unit that separates growth from efficiency — Dana's actual question.

**Inputs.** `decision_record` GPU-seconds per tier, `pool_manifest`, declared cost parameters.
**Outputs.** Per-request cost; the horizontal axis of every frontier; `savings_ledger` rows.

**Key design choice — every cost parameter is declared, versioned, stored on the `frontier_run`, and editable in the UI with the curve re-rendering live.** A vendor whose saving survives the customer pushing the multiplier to 5× has a different conversation from one whose saving is defended. Publishing the derivation is the unclaimed contribution [S7] identifies by omission; publishing a frontier with an undeclared cost axis reproduces exactly the incomparability [S13] complains of.

**Failure modes.** Wrong utilisation → the single largest error source, addressed by measuring rather than asking where the serving stack exposes it. Batching non-linearity → per-token cost depends on concurrency; ~$0.10/M tokens on a batched H100 assumes high concurrency [S26], so the axis records the batch regime it was measured under. **Savings quoted against raw GPU cost** → the most damaging failure, since it inflates the number 3–5× and is the exact error a customer like Marcus catches; the multiplier is a required, non-defaulted field.

## DD5 — The difficulty classifier (the ablation)

**Goal.** Predict pre-generation which tier resolves a request, dispatching once and paying no failed attempt.

**Algorithmic approach.** The field's own families, per [S6]'s taxonomy: **BERT-classifier router** (W1-27), **causal-LLM router** (W1-26), **matrix factorisation** (W1-25), **Bradley–Terry win-probability** (W1-24), **similarity-weighted ranking** (W1-28), **kNN over query embeddings** (W1-29). Trained per deployment on `judgment_record` labels via the **label recycler**. Evaluated with **item response theory** (W2-7) so query difficulty and tier ability are separated rather than entangled.

**Inputs.** Prompt-derived features — **no free-text feature is permitted** (O8) — endpoint, length band, historical labels.
**Outputs.** Predicted tier + confidence onto the `decision_record`; reported ceiling-recovery fraction (M2).

**Key design choice — it is reported as an ablation against calibrated confidence, never against a fixed-model baseline** (PR3). Beating a fixed model is table stakes; [S8] finds simple confidence measures route as well as trained routers, so calibrated confidence is the only honest comparison arm. And **CAMIR does not claim classifier superiority at all** (N7): [S4] benchmarks 21 methods across 5 benchmarks, finds them converged in a narrow band far below oracle from a *predictability bottleneck*, and reports remedies buying up to 2.13 percentage points.

**The one credible escape, on the roadmap and labelled research risk.** **Prefill-activation routing** (W3-1) and **linear probes for correctness prediction** (W3-2) read the model's internal state rather than the prompt's surface [S10] — the signal class self-hosting makes available and hosted-API routers structurally cannot reach. It is the only asymmetry CAMIR has against the predictability bottleneck, and it is unproven.

**Failure modes.** No lift over calibrated confidence → **the expected result**; cascade stays primary and the null is published. Distribution shift → prompt-embedding drift tests (W2-44) and prequential accuracy (W2-46). Label leakage from the recycler → production labels come from the same gate the classifier feeds, so held-out splits must be time-based, never random.

## DD6 — Counterfactual savings attribution

**Goal.** Compute what the fixed-model baseline would have cost **on this exact traffic**, in code the customer can re-run without CAMIR's cooperation.

**Algorithmic approach.** **Counterfactual baseline replay** (W2-22) as ground truth where affordable: re-run a sampled share of production requests through the baseline tier and compare directly. Where it is not affordable, **off-policy estimation** — **inverse propensity scoring** (W2-23), **self-normalised IPS** (W2-24), and a **doubly robust estimator** (W2-25) as the default because it is unbiased if *either* the propensity model or the outcome model is right. **Interleaved/switchback assignment** (W2-26) where a live holdout is acceptable, which is the only design that survives a skeptic entirely. **CUPED** (W3-41) to reduce variance using pre-period cost as a covariate. Framed by **FinOps counterfactual accounting** (W3-31) and, crucially, **eligible-savings definition as a contract term** (W3-30) — [S38][S39] show that in share-of-savings FinOps the percentage is never the negotiation; *what counts as eligible savings* is.

**Inputs.** `decision_record`, `savings_ledger`, cost parameters (DD4), holdout assignment.
**Outputs.** `savings_ledger` rows; the savings report; the number Dana signs against.

**Key design choice — this computation lives in the **open** half of the codebase, permanently** (O6, G3). Marcus's objection is *"every vendor tells me they saved me money — they're also the ones doing the math."* No amount of methodology fixes that; only the vendor not being the computing party does. It is also why CAMIR does not price on share-of-savings despite the FinOps precedent — see [../financials/pricing.md](../financials/pricing.md).

**Failure modes.** Deterministic routing → no propensity variation, so IPS is undefined; a small randomised exploration fraction is required and its cost is a declared line. Cache interaction → routing prices only cache-miss traffic, which is adversely selected toward the hard end [S36][G4]; **cache-miss-conditioned frontier estimation** (W2-48) and **selection-model correction** (W2-50) are the corrections, and both are unvalidated. Baseline drift → the "before" changes for reasons unrelated to routing, which is why the switchback design exists.

## DD7 — Drift detection and recalibration triggering

**Goal.** Decide when the frontier a policy was signed against is no longer true — and never re-fit silently under a signed policy.

**Algorithmic approach.** **Statistical process control**, imported wholesale because it is the mature discipline for exactly this: **Shewhart charts on measured quality** (W3-34), **CUSUM** (W3-35) for small persistent shifts, **EWMA on escalation rate** (W3-36), and **run rules for alert suppression** (W3-37) so the tolerance owner is not paged by noise. Plus **change detection on escalation rate** (W2-45), **prompt-embedding distribution-shift tests** (W2-44), **prequential accuracy tracking** (W2-46), and **version-pinned recalibration triggers** (W2-47) — a `pool_manifest` change fires a re-run unconditionally, because a model upgrade invalidates the frontier by definition [S29].

**Inputs.** Streaming `decision_record` and `judgment_record`, current `frontier_run`, `pool_manifest` version.
**Outputs.** Recalibration schedule, tolerance breach alerts, `frontier_diff` with every tolerance point projected onto both curves.

**Key design choice — detection and action are separated, and action is announced.** A drift signal schedules a recalibration and tells the owner; it does not silently re-fit a threshold. The policy is an append-only record with a named owner and the `frontier_run` it was set against (O3); a router that quietly re-fits under it has broken the only promise that got it past Ravi.

**Failure modes.** Alert fatigue → run rules and per-endpoint sensitivity; an owner who mutes alerts is a dead deployment. **Recalibration invalidating a signed policy** → the frontier diff exists precisely so the owner sees whether their chosen point moved. Drift undetectable without labels → production judging is sampled, so the monitor runs on escalation rate and calibration error, which are label-free, with quality confirmed on a sampled basis only.

## DD8 — Replay corpus construction

**Goal.** Choose the few thousand requests whose ceiling generalises to the millions that were not measured.

**Algorithmic approach.** **Stratified sampling with per-stratum error bars** (W1-48) across endpoint × length band as the default; **difficulty stratification by oracle label** (W1-47) once a first ceiling exists, since the second corpus can be stratified on the thing that matters rather than on a proxy; **Rasch difficulty calibration for corpus design** (W2-10) to keep the corpus spanning the difficulty range rather than piling on the mode. The corpus is **hashed and pinned** into every `frontier_run`, and **cache-miss-conditioned sampling** (W2-48) applies where a semantic cache sits upstream.

**Inputs.** Customer request logs.
**Outputs.** Pinned, hashed corpus; per-stratum counts and error bars.

**Key design choice — sample size is chosen from a stated precision target and the reason is printed** (*"1,200 gives ±3pp on the ceiling estimate at your traffic's variance"*), not from a round number. The ceiling's error bar is what makes a disqualification defensible; a ceiling with an unstated interval cannot support "do not deploy."

**Failure modes.** **Length as a difficulty proxy fails** — Wen's traffic is the documented case, and W1-32/W1-33 are in wave 1 specifically as *documented failures*; the fallback is difficulty-band sampling after the first ceiling. Logs lacking outcomes → the ceiling is judged rather than observed, which is DD3's cost. Corpus staleness → traffic mix moves; the recalibration scheduler re-draws, and the old hash stays on the old `frontier_run` so nothing is ever silently compared across corpora.

## What is deliberately absent

**Fine-tuning the small tier** (N1) — it raises the ceiling and confounds the measurement. **A serving engine** — vLLM and SGLang exist and routing is being absorbed into them [S11]; CAMIR sits above. **A caching layer** (N3) — upstream, orthogonal [S36], and building it would let CAMIR bank cache savings as routing savings, the exact incentive problem this pack accuses incumbents of. **Multi-turn state** (N5) — the decision unit is one request, as [S6] scopes the field.

## Recommended next 3

1. **Build DD8 → DD1 → DD3 as one shippable unit before any router code exists.** That chain produces the disqualification verdict and the artifact decomposition — the pack's contribution and its only defensible day-two claim — and it returns a *negative* result cheaply, which no other sequence does. DD2 and DD5 are worthless if DD1 says the ceiling is 22%.
2. **Implement conformal risk control (W2-2/W2-3) in DD2 from the first version, not as a later upgrade.** It is the only technique in the stack that converts a declared tolerance into a finite-sample guarantee, and the tolerance-ownership promise made to Ravi in [../product/journeys/beachhead.md](../product/journeys/beachhead.md) is otherwise a heuristic threshold wearing a policy's clothes.
3. **Write DD4's derivation as a public document before the first `frontier_run` ships.** The utilisation figure and the 3–5× all-in multiplier [S27] change the answer by more than any routing improvement will; the derivation is an unclaimed contribution [S7], and publishing it is what makes CAMIR's curves comparable to anyone else's — which is the standing the measurement-layer strategy in [../strategy/market_type.md](../strategy/market_type.md) depends on.
